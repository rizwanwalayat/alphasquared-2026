Trust & process

How we protect the work, and you.

Early on, a client asked us for an SLA document we didn't have. We don't want that call again, and you shouldn't have to ask for any of this either — it's below, before you need it.

Seven things you'd otherwise have to ask for

Documented,
not improvised.

A mutual NDA is standard before we go into specifics about your system, your data or your roadmap — ours, or one your legal team prefers to use instead. Either is fine; we just don't talk scope without one in place.

We collect what you give us through the contact form or a discovery call, and standard site analytics. We use it to respond to your enquiry and, if you engage us, to deliver the project. We don't sell it, and we don't hand it to a third party for their own marketing. Ask info@alphasquared.co to see or remove what we hold on you.

Least-privilege access on every engagement, credentials scoped per client rather than shared across projects, encrypted transit end to end, and secrets kept out of shared repositories. Staging and production stay separate environments, and every change is reviewed before it merges — the same discipline this site itself ships with, down to its transport headers.

Discover, design, build, operate — the same four steps on every engagement. Iterative delivery with a working demo every week, version control and code review on every change, and a staging environment that mirrors production before anything ships to it.

Support after launch is part of the engagement, not an upsell we spring on you afterward. What that looks like — a retainer, hourly as-needed, or a dedicated on-call arrangement — gets agreed before launch, along with what's covered and what response times to expect.

Uptime and response-time targets are set per system and put in writing before go-live — not a boilerplate number pasted into a contract. If the system has to run at 2am on a Tuesday, the SLA reflects that before it's live, not after the first incident.

You own the code and the IP we deliver, on final payment. No vendor lock-in, and no holding the repository hostage. It's hosted in a repository you control, or a shared one, whichever you'd rather run.
